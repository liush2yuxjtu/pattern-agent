ASTER / WORKING NOTEBOOK

The decisions behind the dress.

This is a geometric design and construction review tool. It is not a body-fit or cloth-drape simulation.

01 / An honest unfold

Eight conical bodice sectors and eight conical skirt sectors share a 760 mm waist ring. Each is a developable surface. Its flat pattern is computed analytically from the same radii and height; the animation interpolates between those endpoints.

02 / A deliberate starting point

Default finished rings: top 920 mm, waist 760 mm, hem 2400 mm. Bodice height 270 mm; skirt height 780 mm. There is no size standard, wearer measurement, ease allowance, bust shaping, dart, or body scan behind these values.

03 / Construction language

Panels B01–B08 and S01–S08 each cut once. B and S joins pair each panel’s right edge to the next panel’s left edge. W01–W08 join corresponding waist edges. Pair notches sit at the half-length point. Cut contours offset the stitch boundary by the selected allowance; hems use 25 mm. Grain follows the central generator.

04 / What the sample still needs

Center-back joins B04 and S04 are designated for closure review. A zipper opening, facing, lining, boning, waist stay, support, hem easing, and closure extensions are not drafted. A pattern expert must refine those details and fit a toile before production.

05 / What is checked

The application compares 3D and 2D mesh edges and triangle areas, checks all 24 seam pair lengths and coincident endpoints, and validates finite cut contours. Small mesh-chord differences are tessellation error; the underlying conical development is exact. The seam-release gaps and animation are illustrative, not fabric mechanics.

06 / Review & handoff

Use Compare for linked 3D/2D selection. Review notes are stored on this device and can be exported as JSON with the draft dimensions and seam map. Share review copies a URL containing the view and draft settings, but no notes. The full-scale SVG includes a 100 mm calibration square; print at 100%, with no fit-to-page scaling.

The HTML bundles Three.js r180 and works offline in a WebGL2 browser. A 2D fallback remains available when WebGL cannot start. This study contains no analytics, remote fonts, or background network requests.

Technical references: threejs.org/docs/pages/BufferGeometry.html; OrbitControls.html; MeshPhysicalMaterial.html. Three.js is distributed under the MIT license; its license is included in the handoff.
