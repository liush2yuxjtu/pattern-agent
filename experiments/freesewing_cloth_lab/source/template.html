FreeSewing · collision · wrinkle fields

This page really runs FreeSewing Sandy 4.10.2. Its two Bézier waist/hem arcs are sampled directly, mirrored across the cut-on-fold line, and reused for the four printed panels and the simulation material coordinates. Changing length or circle ratio re-drafts the actual package.

Fold angle and wrinkle estimation

The signed dihedral θ is atan2(ê·(n₁×n₂), n₁·n₂), with flat = 0°. Curvature κ = θ / d, where d is the current edge-to-centroid dual spacing, in m⁻¹. Each triangle displays the strongest adjacent magnitude. The 15° face fraction is a mesh-dependent geometric wrinkle proxy. It does not predict permanent creases, ironing recovery or fabric hysteresis. The cloth solver uses XPBD distance/bending constraints; membrane stress is an assumed plane-stress diagnostic and is not a calibrated shell result.

What is simulated

The skirt shell has four unfolded panels, four correctly directed sewn seams and a free reinforced waist edge on the actual MakeHuman mesh. Sandy’s native SVG includes a waistband, but the waistband, closure and hem construction are not simulated. The fixed 1000 mm waist-to-floor value normalizes the requested skirt length; hip measurements do not affect this supported straight-waistband profile.

The useful lesson from one-prompt games is a shared procedural model plus an autonomous loop: generate, run, measure, inspect, correct. A renderer displays the result; a physics solver predicts motion; a pattern model retains the flat material coordinates.

How this file works

The actual FreeSewing Sandy draft supplies all four unfolded skirt panels, their triangle material coordinates, matching seam maps, the 3D cloth mesh and exports. WebAssembly integrates gravity and solves XPBD distance constraints. The four seam pairs share degrees of freedom, preserving the same sewing correspondence under motion. A BVH queries the actual MakeHuman triangles for collision projection; no capsule mannequin substitutes for the mesh.

WebGL2 draws the body and garment using bundled Three.js. The renderer uses procedural weave shading, soft lighting and shadows. The body is anatomical MakeHuman geometry, not a human invented from primitives and not a reconstruction of a particular person. The waist edge uses a mass-weighted shape-matching constraint as a stiff-belt approximation. It has no world/body anchors: translation and rotation remain free. Contact and Coulomb friction (assumed μ=0.6) supply external reactions; gravity remains 9.81 m/s².

Heatmaps and CLO conventions

For each triangle, F = Ds Dm⁻¹ maps 2D material to deformed 3D. Eigenvalues of FᵀF yield principal stretch. 100% means no stretch, matching CLO’s strain convention. Stress is calculated from Green strain with an assumed isotropic plane-stress modulus and Poisson ratio, displayed in kPa. It is an estimated material stress, not avatar pressure and not calibrated CLO output.

CLO’s field guide checks sewing lengths, aligned notches, sewing direction, particle distance, collision thickness and skin offset. This demo uses those observable principles. CLO’s proprietary solver and internal certification logic are not reproduced.

Printing and handoff

SVG and DXF are millimetre geometry with a 100 mm calibration square. Solid lines are sewing lines; dashed outlines are 10 mm seam allowances. A4 printing uses 10 mm margins and physical-size tiles. Print at 100%, with browser headers/footers off. Waist and hem are openings; add a waistband, opening/closure, hem finish and fabric-specific tolerances before cutting a production sample.
